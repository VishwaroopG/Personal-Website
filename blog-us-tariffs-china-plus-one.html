US Tariffs & the China+1 Play: Winners, Losers, and How I Model Them

SEP 27, 2026·10 MIN READ·GLOBAL MACRO NOTE 02

February 2026: US-India reciprocal tariff cut to 18% from 25%, Russian-oil punitive tariff removed. Meanwhile ~60% of US smartphone imports now come from India vs 6% in late 2024. Tariffs are no longer headlines — they are earnings drivers. Here is how I separate real China+1 beneficiaries from story stocks.

1. What actually changed in 2025–26

Three facts matter for equity modelling, not the noise:

  • US-India deal (Feb 2, 2026): Reciprocal tariff 25% → 18%, extra 25% Russia-oil tariff removed, India to cut tariffs on US industrial goods and buy $500bn+ of US energy / ICT / coal. Textiles, leather, jewellery SMEs — key voter-base exporters — got immediate relief.
  • China still penalised: Section 301 / forced-labour / excess-capacity probes keep Chinese electronics at 25–145% depending on HTS code. At 25%+, India TCO is 31% cheaper on a representative assembly ($450k vs $654k landed).
  • Proof of shift: Apple chartered cargo planes in Mar 2025 to move ~$2bn iPhones from India; by May 2025 two-thirds of US smartphone imports were India-origin. Mar–Jun 2026: still ~60%. Clothing did not shift — because India tariffs on apparel ended higher than China. Product-level differentials decide everything.

Analyst lesson: Never say “China+1 benefits India.” Ask: what is the product-level tariff gap between China and India for this HTS code, and does the Indian supplier have certified capacity today?

3. Where India is genuinely ready (2026)

High conviction: EMS, wire harnesses, forgings & castings, precision machining

Foxconn / Tata Electronics / Jabil India for SMT; Motherson / Minda for harnesses; Rajkot-Ludhiana-Pune for forgings; Pune-Coimbatore-Bengaluru for machining. These clear my filter: tariff exposure 25%+, export-mature, qualification 60 days to 6 months.

Selective: auto components, specialty chemicals, technical textiles

Wins where US buyers need friend-shoring for compliance (CFIUS, forced-labour Section 301). But India’s trade deficit with China widened to $112.16bn in FY25-26 — concentrated in electronics, machinery, organics. Component depth is still thin. I model only companies localising BOM, not pure assemblers.

Avoid for now: semiconductors (full-stack), cosmetic-grade plastics

Micron Gujarat OSAT is live, Tata Dholera fab is 2026–27. Real fab output is a 2027+ story. Don’t capitalise 2028 earnings in a 2026 target price.

4. How I build it into valuation

  • Revenue: Only appointed-date / qualified RFQs in base case. Pipeline orders get 30–50% probability weight, not 100%.
  • Margins: Year-1 China+1 margins 200–300 bps below steady-state (overtime, rework, expedited freight — Chennai airport cut clearance 30hrs → 6hrs for Apple for a reason). Normalise from Year-2.
  • Capex: PLI + state incentives (TN, Gujarat, Maharashtra, Karnataka) offset 10–20% of capex, but working capital rises with 8–12 weeks safety stock during dual running.
  • Multiple: No China+1 premium multiple until export revenue >25% of sales for two consecutive quarters. Otherwise you pay for a press release.

5. Portfolio takeaway

The February 18% deal + removal of the Russia-oil surcharge makes India the least-bad alternative, not an automatic winner. PIIE’s warning is right: if new Section 301 tariffs don’t keep a wide China-vs-India gap, supply chains drift back to China on cost. For Indian portfolios, that means overweight certified exporters with US customer audits passed, underweight domestic-only names trading on China+1 narratives, and hedge rupee + crude alongside tariffs — all three hit margins together.
