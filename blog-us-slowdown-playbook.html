US Slowdown Playbook: How I Would Rebalance Indian Portfolios

SEP 27, 2026·10 MIN READ·GLOBAL MACRO NOTE 04

US real GDP slowed to 1.5% in Q2 (from 2.1%), but nominal GDP ran at 8.0% with CPI ~3.2%. The Fed hiked to 3.75–4.00% and may go to 4.25%. Recession odds: 35% (JPM) vs “unusually low” (RBC). For a ₹1,500-cr style mandate, the label matters less than the playbook. Here is mine.

1. Base case: mid-to-late cycle, not recession — yet

Deutsche Bank expects US 2.1% in 2026, 2.0% in 2027; RBC sees mid-to-late cycle with low 12-month recession risk, overweight US / Canada / Asia. AI capex — up to $1tn by top-5 hyperscalers in 2027 — plus ~$2tn deficits keep nominal growth hot even as real cools. S&P earnings were among the strongest in five years; target 8,400. In other words: sticky inflation + strong earnings + elevated yields competing with equities. That is a stock-picker’s regime, and September proved it — Nifty –3.9% on FPI selling, cushioned by ₹52,617 crore of DII buying.

Mandate rule from OneClick experience: never let a macro view override entry hurdles. Rebalance weights and factor tilts; don’t market-time the full ₹1,500 Cr book on a Fed call.

3. The 5 rebalancing levers

(1) Quality over beta — tighten entry screens

Net Debt/EBITDA <1.5x, OCF >70% of EBITDA, ROCE > stressed CoE + 300 bps, funded order-book only. In 2026 this single filter would have avoided most leveraged HAM SPVs and resin-price casualties.

(2) Barbell duration

Pair short-duration cash compounders (diagnostics-like distribution, building-material dealers with pricing power) with select long-duration capex winners where commercialisation is <2 quarters away. Avoid mid-duration stories — capex done, cash flow 3 years out — they suffer most when WACC rises.

(3) Export vs domestic split

Keep 20–30% in certified US exporters (EMS, harnesses, forgings) as a dollar hedge, rest in domestic infra / materials tied to NHAI / Jal Jeevan Mission where award pace is visible. If rupee slides further past 96, exporters cushion dollar NAV.

(4) Primary-market selectivity

FPIs bought ₹5,515 cr of IPOs on Sep 24 while dumping secondaries. Mirror that selectivity: participate only where pricing leaves 20%+ upside on stressed CoE, not scarcity premium.

(5) Cash + rebalance calendar

Hold 5–10% tactical cash in slowdown regime; rebalance quarterly on KPI trackers (order inflow, receivable days, spread trends), not daily on Fed headlines. Add AI-value-chain exposure (energy, data-centre, machinery) on 10%+ drawdowns — DB’s diversification logic — rather than chasing hyperscalers at peak multiples.

4. What would make me pivot

  • Bullish pivot: Fed pauses + US 10Y <4.5% + Brent to $75–80 + rupee stabilises — add small-cap beta, extend duration, trim cash to 0–3%.
  • Bearish pivot: US payrolls break + S&P –15% + Brent >$110 + FPI outflows accelerate past ₹3 lakh cr YTD — raise cash to 15%, cut all net-debt >2x, keep only net-cash + funded-book names.
  • Midterm wildcard (Nov 3, 2026): 66% odds of Democratic sweep per prediction markets. Pre-vote volatility is normal; post-vote clarity usually supports equities, but debt-ceiling brinkmanship and AI-regulation risk argue against pre-positioning aggressively.

The goal is not to predict Washington. It is to build a Mumbai portfolio that survives any Washington outcome — and buys aggressively when others are forced to sell.
