Fed to FII: How US Rates Drive Indian Small & Mid-Caps

SEP 27, 2026·9 MIN READ·GLOBAL MACRO NOTE 01

In September 2026 the Fed hiked to 3.75–4.00%, the US 10-year pushed above 5.10%, and FPIs pulled ₹20,974 crore from Indian equities in 18 days. If you cover Indian small-caps, you cannot model earnings without modelling Washington. Here is the transmission chain I use.

1. The 2026 setup in one paragraph

YTD FPIs have sold ~₹2.45 lakh crore (~$25.5bn) — already 48% more than all of 2025. September alone saw ₹18,531 crore of net selling into the 25th, while DIIs bought ₹52,617 crore / ₹16,398 crore in the week to Sep 25. Nifty fell ~3.9% in September to ~23,140. Rupee touched 95.9–96 per dollar, Brent held above $100 on Middle-East tension, and the September 17 Fed hike was unanimous with dots guiding to one more hike this year.

2. The three channels: yields, currency, risk appetite

Channel A — Relative yield

When risk-free dollars yield 5%+, the India-US yield differential compresses. A global allocator comparing an Indian small-cap at 18x forward earnings to a US investment-grade bond asks a harder question. Cost of equity in my models = US 10Y + India ERP + small-cap illiquidity premium. A 100 bps move in the US 10Y flows straight into WACC and shaves 12–18% off a long-duration small-cap DCF.

Channel B — Currency return erosion

FPIs book returns in dollars. A 5–6% annual rupee depreciation wipes out a large part of a 12% INR return. At 96 per dollar, even DIIs cushioning the index does not help the FPI dollar NAV. This is why secondary-market selling (-₹11,490 crore in Sep 21–25) coexists with selective primary-market buying (+₹5,515 crore on Sep 24 alone): new IPOs with pricing power can clear the currency hurdle; broad beta cannot.

Channel C — Crude + current account

Brent above $100 hits India twice: import bill widens, and input costs (diesel for EPC haulage, petchem feedstock for laminates) squeeze EBITDA. That links Fed policy to my Road and Building Materials coverage directly.

Analyst rule: Before updating any target price in a Fed week, I update three cells first — US 10Y, USD/INR, Brent. Only then earnings.

3. Why small & mid-caps move more than Nifty

Three reasons from my ICICI Securities coverage of 12 small/mid-caps:

  • Ownership float: Lower FPI ownership means when even marginal FPI selling hits, liquidity gaps widen. Bid-ask spreads double in risk-off weeks.
  • Duration: Small-cap value is back-ended — capex today (Naidupeta, Tindivanam-type plants; HAM equity contributions), cash flow in FY28–30. Higher discount rates punish back-ended cash flows most.
  • Pass-through lag: Large-caps pass on fuel and rates faster. Road EPC sub-contractors and laminate distributors absorb 1–2 quarters of margin pain.

5. What to watch next

  1. Fed dots and Chair commentary: One more hike is priced. Any dovish dissent re-steepens EM flows fast — July–August saw ₹49,830 crore return on just pause hopes.
  2. US 10Y 5% level: Sustained break above 5.10% historically triggers FPI secondary selling; fall below 4.5% re-opens small-cap re-rating.
  3. Rupee 96 + Brent $100 combo: If both persist, RBI is constrained, importers guide down margins, and my EBITDA sensitivity tables shift 150–200 bps lower.
  4. Primary vs secondary divergence: Continued FPI IPO participation while selling secondaries = stock-picker's market, not a beta market.

In short: US monetary policy is an input, not background noise, to Indian small-cap valuation. Model it explicitly, stress your WACC, demand stronger balance sheets, and you can buy the DII-cushioned dips with conviction instead of hope.
