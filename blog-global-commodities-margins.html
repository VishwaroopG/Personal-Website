From Crude to Phenol: How Global Commodity Cycles Hit Indian Margins

SEP 27, 2026·9 MIN READ·GLOBAL MACRO NOTE 03

March 2026: phenol at Rs 160/kg (from Rs 85), formaldehyde at Rs 35+ (from Rs 14), melamine at Rs 135 (from Rs 88). June 2026: moderation to Rs 120 / Rs 23-24 / Rs 100 after Hormuz reopened. If you cover laminates, plywood and panels, this swing is your entire earnings revision. Here is how I model it.

1. What happened in 2026 — the textbook shock

West Asia conflict tightened the Strait of Hormuz in Q1 2026. Benzene and propylene — phenol feedstocks — spiked, freight and import availability seized, and India, which consumes ~650 KT of phenol annually growing at 8–9% CAGR (CRISIL), felt it instantly. Phenolic resin hit $1.59/kg in India in August; phenol CIF India was $1,207/MT in June. Some panel makers contemplated production halts; the industry took a unanimous price hike, then needed another just to reach breakeven.

Relief came only when the US-Iran peace understanding reopened Hormuz plus India’s temporary customs-duty exemption on phenol (Apr 2–Jun 30, 2026). Prices softened — but not back to start. That asymmetry is the whole margin story.

2. The chain: Brent → benzene/propylene → phenol → resin → laminate EBITDA

Brent above $100 does not just hurt oil marketers. Cumene → phenol + acetone → phenolic resin → plywood adhesive / laminate. A $10/bbl Brent move typically transmits to benzene with a 3–5 week lag, then to phenol in 4–8 weeks. Laminate makers hold 30–45 days of resin inventory, so Q1 cost shock hits P&L in Q2. That lag is why earnings downgrades cluster one quarter after the commodity headline.

Analyst rule: I track three spreads weekly — phenol-minus-benzene, resin-minus-phenol, laminate realisation-minus-resin. When the third spread compresses two weeks in a row, I cut next-quarter EBITDA 100–150 bps pre-emptively.

3. Why price hikes never fully offset

  • Pass-through is capped: Dealers accept 3–5% quarterly hikes; a 70–90% input spike needs 12–15% realisation. Volumes crack first — PlyReporter noted halt considerations in March.
  • Mix shifts down: In stress quarters buyers trade down from premium laminates to economy / MDF, hurting the high-margin tail that drives SOTP.
  • Working capital balloons: Higher inventory value + slower receivables = OCF conversion drops from ~70% to 40–50%. My models penalise this via higher debt, not just lower EBIT.

4. My sensitivity tree for a Greenlam-type name

Base: EBITDA margin 12%. Shock case (+50% phenol, +100% formaldehyde, 4% price hike only): resin cost/kg +35%, gross margin –280 bps, EBITDA –220 bps after partial pass-through. Relief case (phenol –25% from peak, full hike retained): margin +180 bps — this is the inflection longs pay for. Capex (Naidupeta / Tindivanam-type) amplifies both ways via operating leverage.

5. What to watch now

  1. Hormuz + Brent $90 vs $100: CIOs see Brent averaging ~$90 in Q4, $75–80 in 2027 if de-escalation holds. Sustained $100+ re-tests March highs.
  2. Benzene / propylene weekly: Leads phenol by a month. Q2 2026 softening drove the June relief.
  3. China phenol-acetone run-rates (82–86% in Apr–May): High runs = ample supply = caps upside. New Chinese capacity is the structural lid on 2026–27 prices.
  4. Domestic phenol commissioning (Haldia ~Rs 6,000 cr): Reduces import dependence structurally — the CRISIL $1,000–1,020/T stability thesis for FY26.

Bottom line: in Building Materials, macro is micro. Model commodities as explicitly as volumes, demand proof of pass-through before re-rating, and buy margin troughs only when spreads turn — not when headlines calm.
